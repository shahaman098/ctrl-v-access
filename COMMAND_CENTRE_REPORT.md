### G. Global Winning Research: What Decides First Place

1. **The 30-Second Rule:** Judges make up their minds within the first 30 seconds of the demo video. If the core problem and live solution are not shown immediately, the project loses momentum.
2. **Hard-to-Fake Technical Spine:** Judges with engineering backgrounds (Bridgestone, ScreenMeet) look for deterministic logic and client-side reliability, not just API calls. Demonstrating on-device syllable tokenization, Flesch-Kincaid formula computation, and WCAG AAA compliance proves rigorous implementation.
3. **Action Over Information:** Informing a user that their disability claim was denied has low utility. Generating a structured, citation-backed Mandatory Reconsideration letter that directly forces an administrative reversal turns passive information into real-world power.

### H. Evidence-Backed Problem Opportunity Map

1. **Problem 1: The Healthcare Denial Wall (Critical Impact)**
   - *Evidence:* Millions of patients receive clinical funding denials (e.g. for Continuous Glucose Monitors, specialist therapies) written in dense medical coding (Grade 16+). Over 60% of appeals succeed, but few patients understand how to cite statutory guidance (e.g., NICE NG17).
2. **Problem 2: Disability Benefits Gatekeeping (Critical Impact)**
   - *Evidence:* Assessment boards (e.g. DWP PIP, SSI) award 0 points based on brief phone calls, ignoring chronic neuropathic conditions. Legal statutory reliability rules (e.g. Regulation 4(2A) "safely, repeatedly, reliably") are omitted from decision letters.
3. **Problem 3: Academic Accommodation Bureaucracy (High Impact)**
   - *Evidence:* University disability boards reject extra time and assistive technology by asserting arbitrary recency rules on permanent neurological conditions (ADHD, dyslexia), violating statutory anticipatory duties.
4. **Problem 4: Housing Assistance Paperwork Lapses (Critical Impact)**
   - *Evidence:* Tenants on housing subsidies face sudden voucher termination notices due to paperwork delays, unaware that requesting an informal hearing legally stays the eviction.
